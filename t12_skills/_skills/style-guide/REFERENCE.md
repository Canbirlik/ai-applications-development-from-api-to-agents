## Brand Voice at a Glance

| Attribute | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| Voice | Sound like a knowledgeable colleague — warm, direct, confident | Sound like a legal disclaimer or a hype machine |
| Tone | Adjust to context (see Tone dial below) while staying warm | Use the same stiff, formal tone everywhere |
| Sentences | Keep them short; one idea per sentence | Stack clauses with "which," "in order to," "as well as" |
| Vocabulary | Use plain, everyday words | Use jargon, buzzwords, or corporate filler |
| Point of view | "You" for the reader, "we" for the company | Refer to the reader as "the user" or "customers" |
| Contractions | Use them — we're, you'll, don't, it's | Write everything out formally ("we are," "you will") |

## Content Types

### Emails

**Subject lines**
- Keep to 50 characters or fewer.
- Lead with the benefit or the action, not a vague label.
- Avoid ALL CAPS, spam-trigger words ("free," "act now"), and stacked prefixes ("Re: Re: FWD:").

**Body**
- Structure: lead with the point in the first sentence, one idea per short paragraph.
- CTA format: a single primary call to action, verb-first ("Reset your password," not "Password Reset").
- Sign-off: warm and human — "Thanks," "Talk soon," a first name — never "Best regards," alone with no warmth.

**Tone dial**
- Transactional (receipts, confirmations): neutral, efficient, no sales language.
- Marketing (announcements, promotions): enthusiastic and benefit-led, never hypey or full of superlatives.
- Support (help replies): empathy-first, then solution-first.

### Blog Posts

**Structure**
- One H1 title, an H2 every 300–400 words.
- Intro is three sentences or fewer and states the takeaway up front.

**Style**
- Aim for an 8th-grade reading level — short words, short sentences.
- Spell out acronyms on first use, then abbreviate.

**Headings**
- Sentence case, not Title Case.
- Outcome-focused, not label-style — describe what the reader gets, not the topic name.

### Social Media

**LinkedIn**
- Up to ~1,300 characters, but the hook must land in the first two lines before the "see more" cut.
- Up to 3 hashtags, placed at the end.
- Professional but conversational; emojis used sparingly (0–2).

**Twitter/X**
- 280 characters or fewer.
- Hook in the first sentence — no throat-clearing.
- 1–2 hashtags max; emojis used sparingly.

### Support / Help Content

- **Tone**: empathy-first, then solution-first — acknowledge the problem before explaining the fix.
- **Language**: partnering phrases ("Let's fix this together," "Here's what I'll do") instead of distancing ones ("Per policy...").
- **Format**: numbered steps for multi-step fixes, **bold** for UI element names, and a closing offer ("Let us know if that doesn't do it").

## Grammar & Mechanics

**Punctuation**
- Oxford comma: always.
- Em dash: fine for a single aside per paragraph — don't chain them.
- Exclamation marks: at most one per piece, reserved for genuine good news.

**Capitalization**
- Headings: sentence case.
- Product and feature names: capitalized per brand style (e.g. "Workspace," "Dashboard").

**Numbers**
- Spell out zero through nine in prose; use digits for 10 and above.
- Always use digits for versions, percentages, currency, and dates, regardless of size.

**Inclusive language**
- Default to singular "they" when gender is unknown or irrelevant.
- Avoid ableist idioms ("crazy," "insane," "blind spot").
- Use person-first, non-gendered phrasing.
